## Do this

- line-of-fire safety
- stop stored-energy work
- prevent concealment
- preserve tendon-specific evidence
- bound adjacent and repeated tendons
- retrieve PT failure signatures
- compare approved records and field evidence
- calculate documented elongation variance without accepting it
- create stored-energy holds and exclusion-zone prompts
- route to PT-qualified decision makers

## Never do

- stressing or detensioning authorization
- elongation acceptance
- drill or cut clearance
- repair or grout design
- structural loading release
- stressing from memory or pressure alone
- restressing an out-of-range tendon without disposition
- working behind a live end, fixed end, jack, or ejection path
- cutting a tail before elongation acceptance
- drilling or cutting from drawings or scanning alone without the project permit
- patching a blowout or grout void before bounding it
- assuming a tendon is safe because it appears loose or was reportedly detensioned
